They say a guest should bring something to the house.
I brought a degree, a company, a clean shave.
I left the rest of myself in Lagos
where i keep it.

I dressed for you the night i met your parents.
Button-up. Clean cut. Perfume.
Not to look handsome. To look expensive.
To look educated, arriving, safe,
to build a shield tall enough
that a person might see the shield first
and the skin second, or later, or never.

Your mother was kind. Your father was polite.
No one said a word against me.
But the questions came at a certain angle,
circling, gentle, curious,
the questions of people working honestly
at a difference they had not solved,
and i answered every one of them well.
I have always answered well.

Here is what i did not say.

I did not say that we ran the streets
guiding a bald rubber tire with a stick
for the whole length of an afternoon,
or that the yellow lid of a custard tin
was a wheel, a shield, a currency among boys.

I did not say that we walked home from school for miles
eating smoked fish and stewed yam
folded into newspaper, and that the ink
of yesterday's headlines got into the oil,
and that i have eaten in good restaurants since
in three or four countries
and remember none of them as clearly.

I did not say that we crossed the gutter
to the woman with the makeshift kitchen,
twenty naira for beans in palm oil stew,
two meat if the day was good,
five naira for a sachet of cold water
that you bit open at the corner
and drank with your head tipped back like a king.

I did not say that when NEPA gave the light back
the whole house shouted like a goal had been scored,
and we ran inside to catch whatever remained
of our programme on AIT, on NTA,
praying to reach the end of it
before the dark came back for its property.

I did not say that i walked to the filling station
for one hundred naira of petrol
so the generator could hold the fans through the night,
and that a boy carrying fuel home for his brothers
feels, at eleven years old, like a provider.

None of this is poverty when you are inside it.
It only becomes poverty when you try to say it
at a table with cloth napkins.

So i did not say it.
I said Playgrounds. I said research. I said vision.
I said the words that have passports.

And behind that, the arithmetic i cannot stop.
Is this a season for her, a door she is trying,
and am i the door.
Will they accept me, and if they smile and do not,
will the not arrive later, quietly, in her ear.
They gave me no evidence.
I have never needed evidence.
The counting does not wait for proof,
it only waits for a room.

I am not accusing your family.
They were decent people meeting a stranger
and doing the ordinary work of curiosity.
The trouble was never entirely in that room.
Some of it walked in wearing my shirt.

And you were kind. Let me say that plainly.
You never asked me to leave anything at the door.
You would have listened to the tire and the stick,
the custard lid, the sachet of cold water,
the whole red street of it.
I never offered them.
That was not your doing.

A man cannot be known
by people he has never introduced himself to.
I have spent years being liked
by rooms that never met me.

The plates were cleared. Someone asked me
about the company, about where it goes from here,
and i set down my glass
and gave them the good answer,
the practiced one,
the one that has never once failed me.

Somewhere behind my teeth
a boy was still running down a red street
with a stick and a rolling tire,
laughing at nothing,
waiting to be introduced.
