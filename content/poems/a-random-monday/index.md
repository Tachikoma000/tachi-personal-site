It is July. The year has five months left in it.
I am writing from inside the thing,
which is not where I write from.
I do not know how this ends.
That is the entire reason it is being written now.

Most mornings I open the laptop
before my eyes are properly open
and I read the announcements.
Not for news. For one specific sentence
that has not appeared yet.

Because this is the shape of the fear.
Not a competitor. Not a bad quarter.
Some ordinary Monday, a company
with more people in one team than I have in the world
ships a feature.
It is free. It is a checkbox in a menu.
It is the five years.

Here is what we have, honestly counted.

Rig is out in the world and people build on it,
and it is open source, which means
the thing I am proudest of has no price on it.
I cannot put it in a column.
A million and a half downloads
is a number that means everything to me
and nothing at all to a man with a term sheet.

Ryzome is young. Some people pay for it.
Not enough people pay for it.

Arc made real money and crypto does not hold still
long enough for anyone to value it,
and I would not build a company on a thing
that moves like that.

So we have tried to raise, and it has not landed.
Not once. Not twice.
And every time, the honest answer was the same,
that we were not ready,
and I have said that sentence to myself so often
I can no longer hear
whether it is a diagnosis or a excuse
wearing a diagnosis to work.

Fourteen people.

Fourteen people arranged their lives
around a conviction that came out of my mouth.
They could be anywhere. The market is good for them.
They are here.

I think most days that I am not doing enough for them.
I do not mean that as confession. I mean it as arithmetic.
There is a version of this company
with a better man at the front of it
and I can see that version from where I stand.

And at three in the morning it is never the money.
The money has a spreadsheet and the spreadsheet has a date.
The three o'clock version is smaller and worse.
It asks whether the person at the head of that table
belongs at the head of that table.
It asks it patiently. It has all night.

I have already written the admission.
I keep it drafted, the way you keep a will.
It says, I was not ready to build in this space.
It says, my conviction was flawed
and no matter how hard we worked
we could not reach the people we made it for.
It says, I tried, and I grew,
and vision and execution are two different animals
and I may have only ever had the first one.

I have put down a word before.
One was given to me at nine years old, in my own voice,
and I carried it twenty-three years
and set it down in the middle of a road.
But that word had been spoken.
That is the thing about it. It existed.
A man can put down a thing that exists.

Nobody has said this one to me.
There is no sentence yet.
There is only a sentence waiting in December
to find out whether it gets to be true.

So we go again at the end of the year,
when the numbers say something a stranger can read.
Ryzome will find its people or it will not.
I believe it will. I want that on the page
in case I am right, and in case I am not.

Tomorrow I will open the laptop
before my eyes are properly open,
and the sentence will not be there,
and I will feel the small ridiculous relief
of a man granted one more day
of being someone who might be right.

Then I will go to work.
Fourteen people will go to work.
None of us will know.
We will do it anyway,
which I am told is the job,
and which nobody told me
would feel exactly like this.
