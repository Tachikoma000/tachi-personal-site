Before anything else, her.
Because what follows will sound like a man
assembling a defence,
and there is no defence. There is only what happened.

She cooked like I was somebody's king.
She was loyal in a way I have not met since.
She laughed at the stupid things I said
and could make me useless with laughing.
No one has loved me the way that woman loved me.
Put that at the top of the page
and do not let me take it down.

There was a thing our life did not have.
The reasons were hers and they were not her fault
and they are not mine to set down here.
Understand only that it was never refusal.
Nobody in that house was withholding anything.

So we found the other doors, and there were many.
Hands. The long kiss at the door.
Her head on my chest with the whole weight of it,
which I loved and still love,
being the warmer body, the one that gives the heat.
Her legs wound into mine in the dark.
For a long season I believed that was enough.
For a long season it was.

Then I began to notice what I had become.
Provider first. Then driver. Then fixer.
Then the one who called about the insurance.
Then confidant. Then something like a therapist,
which no man should be to the woman he lies beside.
Then, near the end, something close to a roommate.

Somewhere in that list the boyfriend was retired.
Nobody held a ceremony.
There was no last day, no notice given.
He simply stopped appearing on the schedule.

I have a big body. Long arms. Long hands.
It is a good body for holding a person
and it was not being asked for anything else.

A man can go a long way
convincing himself he is above the wanting,
that love is the high thing and the body only a guest.
Then some ordinary afternoon
a stranger passes close to him in a doorway
and his whole skin reports for duty,
and he learns that he has been lying,
and that he has been lying for years,
and that the one he lied to hardest
was the one doing the lying.

And I said nothing.

That is the part I own.
Not the leaving. The years standing in front of the leaving.
When I was hurt I smiled and negotiated it away.
When I was angry I went quiet
and let the silence do the punishing for me.
I have never once said early, plainly, to a woman's face,
this is what I need and I am not receiving it.
I could not say it to her.
I am not certain I could say it now.

And she was carrying weather of her own,
older than me and heavier than mine.
When the dark came for her I learned its timetable.
When she went under, I went under.
By the last year I could not tell
which of us a feeling had started in.
A man cannot pull anyone out of a room
he has already moved into.

So I ended it, and I ended it badly.
Some months apart, I said. We will revisit it.
Both of us knowing, or at least me knowing,
that revisit was a soft word wearing a coat.

She called. We talked. We cried down the telephone.
She asked to see me and I said I was not ready.
I was never going to be ready.
I let her hold that door open a whole season
because I did not have the courage
to close it while she was watching.

Then she closed it herself.
She did the honest thing I could not do,
and she did it alone.

Some nights I put it back on the scale.
The bad months. The tempers. The year
I could not separate my sadness from hers.
And against all of that, the meals, the loyalty,
the laughing, the hand that always found mine,
a woman who loved me completely
and asked for nothing except that I stay.

Could I have lived there? Everything but the one thing?
I have run it many times.
It comes out differently depending on the night.

Here is what I have, and it is not much.
I was loved better than I have been loved since
by a woman I could not tell the truth to.
I called my silence kindness for years
and then I called my leaving kindness too,
and only one of those can be true.

She might know which.
I have no way to ask.
